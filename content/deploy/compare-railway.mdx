# Railway alternative: Faable Deploy vs Railway

**Short answer:** Railway runs your services and databases from a visual canvas and bills what they consume, by the second. Faable Deploy keeps the same push-to-deploy workflow and charges **a fixed monthly fee per project**, so the invoice doesn't depend on how busy last month was. Its **free plan includes custom domains** (Railway's has none), every app sits behind a **built-in Web Application Firewall**, **[Faable Auth](../auth/get-started.md) is in the same subscription**, and **your apps and data are hosted in the EU** by a European company. Choosing between Railway and Render as well? There's a [three-way comparison](#railway-vs-render-vs-faable) below.

> Railway and Render details checked against their pricing pages and docs in November 2026. They change often, so confirm anything decisive on their own sites.

## Why teams look for a Railway alternative

### A bill that doesn't move with usage

Railway's plans are a monthly fee that doubles as usage credit: $5 on Hobby and $20 per workspace on Pro. Everything the services consume beyond that is billed per second. That's roughly $20 per vCPU and $10 per GB of memory a month, plus $0.05 per GB of egress and $0.15 per GB of volume storage. It's fair, and it means the invoice reflects whatever your services did last month. You can set a hard usage limit (from $10), but when you reach it "all your workloads will be taken offline".

Faable charges **a fixed fee per plan, per project, never per user**. Hobby is **15 €/month** and covers its compute: each app runs on 1 vCPU and 1.5 GB, with unlimited deployments and 50 GB of egress. On Free and Hobby there is no per-GB line at all, and going over the traffic allowance sends warnings at 80 %, 90 % and 100 % instead of taking your site down. The only metered extras are the ones you switch on yourself, such as always-on apps and workers past their monthly allowance. See [Deploy pricing](pricing.mdx).

### A free plan you can put a domain on

Railway's Free plan gives you $1 of usage a month for one project with up to three services, and **no custom domains**. Faable's [Free plan](pricing.mdx) is permanent and needs no card. It includes **up to 3 projects with custom domains and free SSL**, 50 deployments a day and 10 GB of egress a month, and it allows commercial use. An idle app sleeps after 30 minutes and wakes in a few seconds.

### Security and identity are included

Every Faable app sits behind a [managed Web Application Firewall](security-waf.md). It blocks requests for `.env` files, Git metadata, credentials and admin panels before they reach your code, on every plan, and you can add your own rules. On Railway that is something you put in front yourself. When your app needs sign-in, [Faable Auth](../auth/get-started.md) is already in the subscription: hosted login, social and passwordless sign-in, MFA, passkeys and machine-to-machine tokens.

### European by construction

Picking a European region on a US platform keeps your data's location in Europe. The company that operates it, and the law that can reach it, stays American. Faable is a European company: **your apps and data are hosted in the EU**, with hosting and identity under one provider and one data-processing agreement. More in [European alternatives to Vercel, Render and Railway](european-alternatives.mdx).

## When Railway might fit better

- **You want databases next to your app from the same provider.** Railway runs Postgres, MySQL, Redis and others with persistent volumes on the same canvas. Faable apps are stateless and connect to a [managed database](guides/databases.md) such as Neon or Turso.
- **You like assembling services visually** and want pure usage-based billing that scales down to cents.
- **You need very large instances.** Railway Pro goes up to 1,000 vCPU and 1 TB per service; Faable's largest instance is 6 vCPU · 6 GB, on request.

**The reading:** Render is easier to predict than Railway, since each instance has a price, and its free web services include custom domains, though they take about a minute to wake. Railway is the more flexible of the two and can be the cheapest for small, bursty workloads, but its bill follows usage. Faable is the option where the price stays fixed, the free plan stays usable for real projects, and the firewall, sign-in and European hosting come in the same plan.

## Moving from Railway

A Railway service that builds from your repository moves without code changes. Connect the same repository to Faable, copy the service variables into [environment variables](environment.mdx), make sure your server listens on the `PORT` Faable provides, and push. Databases stay where they are, or move to a [managed database](guides/databases.md), and your app connects with the same connection string. Point your [custom domain](domains/custom-domain.md) at Faable when the new deploy is live.
