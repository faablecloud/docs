# Vercel alternative: Faable Deploy vs Vercel

**Short answer:** Vercel is the best-known home for Next.js: push to Git, get a deploy on a global CDN, with serverless functions for the dynamic parts. Faable Deploy keeps the same push-to-deploy workflow and differs where teams usually start looking elsewhere. Its **free plan allows commercial use**, while Vercel's Hobby is for personal projects only. Your code runs as a **real, long-running server**, not a function with a time limit. You pay a **fixed monthly fee per project**, not per seat plus usage. And **[Faable Auth](../auth/get-started.md) is included**, with **apps and data hosted in Europe** by a European company.

> Vercel details checked against vercel.com pricing, plan docs and limits in November 2026. They change often, so confirm anything decisive on their own site.

## Why teams look for a Vercel alternative

### The free plan has to stay personal

Vercel's terms are explicit: "Hobby teams are for non-commercial personal use only", and "all commercial usage requires a Pro or Enterprise plan". Commercial use includes taking payments, showing ads, and paid or affiliate work. So a client's landing page, an indie product with a Stripe checkout, or a blog with AdSense has to move to Pro, however little traffic it gets.

Faable's [Free plan](pricing.mdx) **allows commercial use**. It is permanent, needs no credit card, includes custom domains, and runs each app with up to 0.6 vCPU and 512 MB. An idle app sleeps after 30 minutes and wakes in a few seconds. Static sites are served directly, with no container, so they never have a cold start.

### Your backend is a server, not a function

On Vercel, the dynamic parts of your app run as functions: up to 300 seconds on Hobby and 800 seconds on Pro, with 2 GB of memory by default. That suits request–response pages. It gets awkward for the things a product backend does all day, like consuming a queue, holding a connection open, or running a job that takes a while.

On Faable your code runs as **an ordinary server process** in a Linux container: Next.js with `next start`, Express, Fastify, NestJS, Django, FastAPI, Flask, Laravel or plain PHP, all detected and built without configuration. There's no per-invocation limit to design around. The only timeout is 60 seconds to the first byte, and a response can then stream for minutes, which is what LLM apps need (see [Hosting AI agents in Europe](ai-agents.mdx)). On Hobby and Pro, a process with no web server at all can run as a [background worker](always-on-and-workers.md), and commands can run on a cron schedule as [scheduled tasks](scheduled-tasks.md), even while the app sleeps.

### A bill you can predict

Vercel Pro is $20 a month for one deploying seat, with $20 of usage credit. Every extra Owner or Member is another $20. Usage beyond the credit (data transfer, CDN requests, function invocations, CPU time and build minutes) is billed on demand, uncapped unless you configure a spend limit. New teams start with a $200 on-demand budget.

Faable charges a **fixed fee per plan, per project**: Hobby is **15 €/month** and Pro **99 €/month**, with no per-seat charge. On Free and Hobby there is no per-GB line at all: the traffic allowance is what the plan includes, and going over it sends warnings at 80 %, 90 % and 100 % instead of opening a meter. Only Pro meters egress, beyond its 100 GB. See [Deploy pricing](pricing.mdx).

### Auth is in the same subscription

Most apps on Vercel add a second vendor for sign-in, such as Clerk, Auth0 or Cognito, with its own bill and its own data-processing agreement. On Faable, [Faable Auth](../auth/get-started.md) is part of the plan: hosted login, social and passwordless sign-in, MFA, passkeys and machine-to-machine tokens, with **15,000 monthly active users on Free** and no MAU limit on Hobby and Pro.

### European by construction

Vercel runs functions in Washington, D.C. (`iad1`) unless you pick another region. Even with a European region, the company operating your app, and the law that can reach it, stays American. Faable is a European company: **your apps and data are hosted in the EU**, and hosting and identity sit under one European provider and one data-processing agreement. More in [European alternatives to Vercel, Render and Railway](european-alternatives.mdx).

### A firewall that closes the obvious doors for you

Both platforms filter traffic at the edge on every plan. Vercel gives you DDoS mitigation and bot protection, plus 3 custom rules on Hobby and 40 on Pro. Faable's [Web Application Firewall](security-waf.md) adds **rulesets we maintain**: requests for `.env` files, Git metadata, credentials, admin panels and PHP probes are blocked before they reach your code or wake a sleeping app. You can add your own rules by path, user-agent or query parameter. It doesn't inspect request bodies, so it isn't a replacement for an OWASP signature engine.

## When Vercel might fit better

- **Your app is Next.js, and you want edge rendering and middleware on a global CDN.** Faable serves from Europe; for a mostly European audience that is closer anyway, but it isn't a worldwide edge network.
- **Your workflow depends on a preview deployment for every pull request.** Faable doesn't create per-branch previews; you can deploy a staging app from [your own CI](github-actions.md).
- **You rely on Vercel-specific products** such as Edge Config, v0 or their analytics and observability suite.

## Moving from Vercel

Most projects move without code changes. Connect the same repository, copy your environment variables, deploy, and point your domain at Faable. Next.js builds with [standalone output](guides/guide-next.md) automatically, so the deployed artifact stays around 150 MB. The step-by-step version, including what changes when serverless functions become a long-running process, is in **[Migrate from Vercel](guides/migrate-from-vercel.mdx)**.
